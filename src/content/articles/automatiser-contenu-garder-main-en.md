An idea sometimes fits into two sentences spoken between other tasks. Turning it into an article still requires sources, an angle, writing, translation and review. I want to automate part of that chain because repetition takes attention. I also want to recognize what I publish.

The end-of-year calendar gave me a useful constraint. I asked for one weekly article in both languages on two sites with different roles. The Unreliable Engineer carries my experiments as an entrepreneur, freelancer and SRE. My professional site focuses more on observability. Distributing the same text everywhere would simplify the pipeline and miss the need.

### The topic remains an editorial decision

The first suggestions were too generic. Then a series about OpAMP occupied too much space. I limited the Rust protocol to one article and kept more room for news and real cases. Those corrections are choices about what I want to say. A style validator cannot make them on my behalf.

It can help prepare a source packet, check translation pairs and flag some expressions that do not sound like me. When a statement rests only on an assumption, I want to see that limitation before it turns into a first-person memory.

### Listening changes my review

For this cycle, I requested one PR per article followed by a reading audio in Media Review. The audio provides another way to review the text. A sentence that looks smooth on screen can become endless when heard; an overly tidy transition can also sound like a brochure.

The review request must remain connected to the exact text. If an agent changes a paragraph after I listen, the earlier version no longer automatically covers that passage. I want to find the feedback, the correction and the resulting version.

### The last stretch stays visible

My rule for this work is explicit: agents may merge after Media Review feedback has been addressed and technical checks pass. An empty review still awaiting input does not count as agreement. This describes the requested process; it does not prove every stage is already automated.

I am trying to remove the manual transport between stages: copying the same brief, finding a version and asking again where an article stands. I keep angle decisions and final review because they determine what I am prepared to publish under my name.
