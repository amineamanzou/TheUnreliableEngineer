When I use my own tool, I bring the problem, the vocabulary and enough patience to work around its limitations. Somebody discovering it has no reason to bring all three.

With FaitSuite and my automation experiments, I want to distinguish two kinds of validation. I can check that a journey works. I still need to understand whether somebody encounters the problem often enough to change how they work. The first result does not replace the second.

### Show a task before showing features

For a meeting tool, I would start with a specific task: finding a decision after the meeting and checking where it came from. I would look at what the person does today, what they lose and what they need to preserve. Their answer might reveal a search problem, a lack of trust in summaries or difficulty sharing with colleagues.

Those three difficulties can produce the same initial request, “I would like to make better use of my meetings,” and lead to different products. Adding a feature after every interview would lengthen the list without necessarily helping me choose.

### The test must be allowed to disappoint me

In a future trial, I want to see whether the person returns with another meeting and retrieves the result without me conducting the demonstration. This is a research intention, rather than an observed outcome. I do not have a return rate or conversion figure to announce in this article.

I also want to understand what prevents usage: installation, privacy, processing delay, correcting names or simply no recurring need. Somebody can enjoy the demonstration and still choose their current method. That decision deserves more attention than a compliment.

### The price includes my support time

Before choosing an offer, I need to account for the time required to make a first use successful. If every new user requires my intervention, that work belongs to the service I provide, even when the software runs on their machine.

I can choose to support early users closely in order to learn. I need to keep that phase visible in my calculation and avoid confusing it with an autonomous product ready to scale.

My next commitment should therefore be a small enough experiment to produce a usable answer: one task, somebody doing it and feedback on what blocks them. The next development decision will depend on that answer, even if it makes part of my backlog unnecessary.
