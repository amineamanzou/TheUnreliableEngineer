While preparing my end-of-year calendar, I find several overlapping projects: FaitSuite, content creation, agent memory and my software factory. Each responds to a different friction. Each can also fill my weeks before I have checked what it actually contributes.

This review is being prepared from my November decisions. I am not pretending to describe December’s results in advance. Before publication, these intentions still need to be compared with what was actually tested, continued or stopped.

### FaitSuite needs usage beyond my own context

I want to take FaitSuite beyond my own setup. The question I am keeping for 2027 concerns returning to the tool: can somebody find their meetings and what they need without me explaining my organization? Privacy and understandable processing states belong to that usage.

I cannot conclude that commercial demand exists because the software solves my own problem. The next step needs to reveal where my need overlaps with other people’s needs and where it remains specific to how I work.

### Automation needs to reduce supervision

For the factory, I want fewer technical stages to require my intervention when I have already provided the rule. Two correction cycles, independent review and explicit validation for decisions that need it provide a framework. I still need to examine restarts, blockers and the cost of supervision.

Content has a similar criterion. I want a chain that prepares, checks and carries versions while leaving me to decide the angle and listen to the text before publication. If I save writing time and spend it finding the correct files, the review needs to reflect that.

### Keeping a project requires evidence

I have not decided a list of projects to abandon here. I want to make that decision with a record of usage, limitations and time spent. A tool I keep for myself can remain a useful experiment. A product I want to commercialize has additional questions to answer.

In 2027, I would therefore like to give more attention to evidence: repeated usage, a restart without a manual reminder, a decision retrieved with its source, user feedback that changes what happens next. The number of active repositories will tell me little if I still do not know which one deserves the next week of work.
